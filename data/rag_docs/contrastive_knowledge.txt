TOPIC: Contrastive Learning
CATEGORY: contrastive

DEFINITION
Contrastive learning trains representations so related examples are close and unrelated examples are farther apart under a chosen similarity objective.

HOW IT WORKS
Positive and negative pairs or views define the training signal.

KEY IDEAS
The exact implementation depends on the model family, data type, objective, and operating constraints.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
embeddings, vision, multimodal learning

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
negative sampling, augmentation, and batch composition matter

COMPARISON
Contrastive Learning should be distinguished from closely related approaches in the same problem family. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what Contrastive Learning is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
