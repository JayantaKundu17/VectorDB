TOPIC: Markov Decision Process
CATEGORY: mdp

DEFINITION
An MDP models sequential decision-making with states, actions, transition probabilities, rewards, and a discount factor.

HOW IT WORKS
The Markov property assumes the current state contains the information needed to predict future transitions and rewards.

KEY IDEAS
The exact implementation depends on the model family, data type, objective, and operating constraints.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
reinforcement learning and planning

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
state design may fail to capture necessary history

COMPARISON
Markov Decision Process should be distinguished from closely related approaches in the same problem family. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what Markov Decision Process is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
