TOPIC: Semantic Search
CATEGORY: semantic_search

DEFINITION
Semantic search retrieves content based on meaning rather than requiring exact term overlap.

HOW IT WORKS
Embedding models map queries and documents into a shared vector space and similarity is used for ranking.

KEY IDEAS
The exact implementation depends on the model family, data type, objective, and operating constraints.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
RAG, knowledge search, recommendation

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
poor embeddings can miss exact identifiers or domain-specific terms

COMPARISON
Semantic Search should be distinguished from closely related approaches in the same problem family. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what Semantic Search is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
