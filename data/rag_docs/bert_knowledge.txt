TOPIC: BERT
CATEGORY: bert

DEFINITION
BERT is an encoder-only transformer pretrained to produce contextual representations and originally trained with masked language modeling.

HOW IT WORKS
Its bidirectional attention makes it useful for understanding-focused tasks such as classification and token labeling.

KEY IDEAS
The exact implementation depends on the model family, data type, objective, and operating constraints.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
classification, retrieval, entity recognition, extractive QA

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
encoder models are not naturally optimized for free-form autoregressive generation

COMPARISON
BERT should be distinguished from GPT. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what BERT is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
