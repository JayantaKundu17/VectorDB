TOPIC: Cross-Validation
CATEGORY: cross_validation

DEFINITION
Cross-validation repeatedly splits training data so models can be evaluated across multiple partitions.

HOW IT WORKS
K-fold CV divides data into k folds and rotates the held-out fold. Time-series problems require order-aware splits.

KEY IDEAS
The exact implementation depends on the model family, data type, objective, and operating constraints.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
model selection on limited data

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
grouped or temporal dependencies require specialized splitting

COMPARISON
Cross-Validation should be distinguished from closely related approaches in the same problem family. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what Cross-Validation is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
