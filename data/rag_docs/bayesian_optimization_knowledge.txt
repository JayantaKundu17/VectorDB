TOPIC: Bayesian Optimization
CATEGORY: bayesian_optimization

DEFINITION
Bayesian optimization uses a surrogate model and an acquisition function to search expensive black-box objectives efficiently.

HOW IT WORKS
A Gaussian process or another surrogate predicts likely promising regions while balancing exploration and exploitation.

KEY IDEAS
The exact implementation depends on the model family, data type, objective, and operating constraints.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
hyperparameter tuning, expensive experiments

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
surrogate-model mismatch, scaling challenges in high dimensions

COMPARISON
Bayesian Optimization should be distinguished from closely related approaches in the same problem family. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what Bayesian Optimization is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
