TOPIC: Experiment Tracking
CATEGORY: experiment_tracking

DEFINITION
Experiment tracking records configurations, data versions, metrics, artifacts, and model outputs so experiments can be reproduced and compared.

HOW IT WORKS
Tools often track parameters, metrics, checkpoints, and source-code or environment information.

KEY IDEAS
The exact implementation depends on the model family, data type, objective, and operating constraints.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
research and production ML workflows

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
incomplete metadata makes experiments irreproducible

COMPARISON
Experiment Tracking should be distinguished from closely related approaches in the same problem family. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what Experiment Tracking is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
