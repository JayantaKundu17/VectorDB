TOPIC: Object Tracking
CATEGORY: tracking

DEFINITION
Object tracking associates detections or image features across frames to estimate persistent object identities and trajectories.

HOW IT WORKS
Tracking can use motion models, appearance features, assignment algorithms, or end-to-end learned systems.

KEY IDEAS
The exact implementation depends on the model family, data type, objective, and operating constraints.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
video analytics, robotics, autonomous driving

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
occlusion, identity switches, missed detections

COMPARISON
Object Tracking should be distinguished from closely related approaches in the same problem family. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what Object Tracking is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
