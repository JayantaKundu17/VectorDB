TOPIC: Clustering
CATEGORY: clustering

DEFINITION
Clustering groups unlabeled observations so points within a group are more similar under a chosen representation or distance.

HOW IT WORKS
Different algorithms encode different notions of cluster shape and density.

KEY IDEAS
The exact implementation depends on the model family, data type, objective, and operating constraints.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
segmentation, exploration, organization

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
there may be no natural clusters and evaluation can be ambiguous

COMPARISON
Clustering should be distinguished from closely related approaches in the same problem family. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what Clustering is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
