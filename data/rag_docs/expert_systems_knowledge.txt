TOPIC: Expert Systems
CATEGORY: expert_systems

DEFINITION
An expert system uses a knowledge base and an inference mechanism to emulate decision-making in a narrow domain.

HOW IT WORKS
A typical system separates facts/rules from an inference engine and may provide explanations for conclusions.

KEY IDEAS
The exact implementation depends on the model family, data type, objective, and operating constraints.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
diagnosis, troubleshooting, compliance rules

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
limited generalization, maintenance burden, rule conflicts

COMPARISON
Expert Systems should be distinguished from closely related approaches in the same problem family. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what Expert Systems is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
