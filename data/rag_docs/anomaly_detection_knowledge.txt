TOPIC: Anomaly Detection
CATEGORY: anomaly_detection

DEFINITION
Anomaly detection identifies observations that differ from the expected pattern.

HOW IT WORKS
Approaches include statistical thresholds, isolation-based methods, density methods, distance methods, and reconstruction error.

KEY IDEAS
The exact implementation depends on the model family, data type, objective, and operating constraints.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
fraud, intrusion, industrial monitoring

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
true anomalies are often rare and labels may be unavailable

COMPARISON
Anomaly Detection should be distinguished from closely related approaches in the same problem family. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what Anomaly Detection is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
