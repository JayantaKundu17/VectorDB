TOPIC: Optimization
CATEGORY: optimization

DEFINITION
Optimization finds parameter values that minimize or maximize an objective function subject to constraints.

HOW IT WORKS
ML training commonly minimizes a differentiable loss with gradient-based methods. Convex and non-convex problems have different guarantees.

KEY IDEAS
The exact implementation depends on the model family, data type, objective, and operating constraints.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
model training, hyperparameter tuning, operations research

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
local minima or saddle points, poor conditioning, expensive search

COMPARISON
Optimization should be distinguished from closely related approaches in the same problem family. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what Optimization is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
