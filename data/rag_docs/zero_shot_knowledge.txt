TOPIC: Zero-Shot Learning
CATEGORY: zero_shot

DEFINITION
Zero-shot learning performs a task or recognizes a class without task-specific labeled examples at inference time.

HOW IT WORKS
Foundation models can use language descriptions or general pretrained knowledge to map novel requests or labels.

KEY IDEAS
The exact implementation depends on the model family, data type, objective, and operating constraints.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
LLMs and multimodal classification

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
performance depends strongly on semantic alignment

COMPARISON
Zero-Shot Learning should be distinguished from closely related approaches in the same problem family. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what Zero-Shot Learning is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
