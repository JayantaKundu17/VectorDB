TOPIC: Chain-of-Thought Prompting
CATEGORY: cot_prompting

DEFINITION
Chain-of-thought prompting asks a model to reason through a problem in intermediate steps, often improving difficult reasoning tasks.

HOW IT WORKS
In production systems, concise rationale or structured verification may be safer than exposing or relying on unrestricted internal reasoning.

KEY IDEAS
The exact implementation depends on the model family, data type, objective, and operating constraints.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
complex reasoning, planning, math

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
reasoning traces can contain mistakes and should not be treated as ground truth

COMPARISON
Chain-of-Thought Prompting should be distinguished from closely related approaches in the same problem family. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what Chain-of-Thought Prompting is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
