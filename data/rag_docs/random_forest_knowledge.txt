TOPIC: Random Forest
CATEGORY: random_forest

DEFINITION
Random forest averages many decision trees trained with randomness in samples or features.

HOW IT WORKS
Bagging and feature subsampling reduce correlation among trees and often improve generalization.

KEY IDEAS
The exact implementation depends on the model family, data type, objective, and operating constraints.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
tabular classification and regression, robust baselines

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
larger models cost more memory and may be less interpretable than a single tree

COMPARISON
Random Forest should be distinguished from bagging and gradient boosting. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what Random Forest is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
