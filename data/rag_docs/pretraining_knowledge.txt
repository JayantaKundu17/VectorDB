TOPIC: Pretraining
CATEGORY: pretraining

DEFINITION
Pretraining learns broad representations or language modeling behavior from large datasets before task-specific adaptation.

HOW IT WORKS
Self-supervised objectives allow training without manually labeled examples for every downstream task.

KEY IDEAS
The exact implementation depends on the model family, data type, objective, and operating constraints.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
foundation models, transfer learning

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
data quality, compute cost, and memorization risks

COMPARISON
Pretraining should be distinguished from closely related approaches in the same problem family. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what Pretraining is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
