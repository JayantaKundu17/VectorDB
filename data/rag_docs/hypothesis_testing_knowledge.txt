TOPIC: Hypothesis Testing
CATEGORY: hypothesis_testing

DEFINITION
Hypothesis testing evaluates whether observed evidence is inconsistent with a null hypothesis under a chosen statistical procedure.

HOW IT WORKS
A test produces a statistic and often a p-value. Statistical significance does not automatically imply practical importance.

KEY IDEAS
The exact implementation depends on the model family, data type, objective, and operating constraints.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
experiments, product comparisons, scientific analysis

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
multiple testing, underpowered studies, p-value misuse

COMPARISON
Hypothesis Testing should be distinguished from closely related approaches in the same problem family. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what Hypothesis Testing is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
