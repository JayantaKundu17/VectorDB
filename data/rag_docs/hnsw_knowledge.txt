TOPIC: HNSW
CATEGORY: hnsw

DEFINITION
HNSW stands for Hierarchical Navigable Small World and is an approximate nearest-neighbor search structure based on a multilayer graph.

HOW IT WORKS
Search begins at a high level, navigates toward promising neighbors, and descends through progressively denser layers.

KEY IDEAS
HNSW uses a hierarchy of graph layers to navigate approximately toward nearby vectors.
The main design variables are representation, objective function, optimization or search procedure, and evaluation criteria.

COMMON USE CASES
high-dimensional similarity search

ADVANTAGES / STRENGTHS
This approach is useful when its assumptions and inductive biases match the structure of the problem. It can provide a strong baseline, specialized performance, interpretability, efficiency, or scalability depending on the topic.

LIMITATIONS / FAILURE MODES
approximation can miss the exact nearest neighbor and memory can be significant

COMPARISON
HNSW should be distinguished from brute-force nearest-neighbor search. The best choice depends on data size, feature type, dimensionality, latency, accuracy, interpretability, and operational constraints.

EVALUATION / PRACTICAL CHECKS
Choose metrics that match the task and business cost of errors. Validate on a representative holdout or cross-validation design, check leakage, inspect failure cases, and monitor performance after deployment.

INTERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Be prepared to explain what HNSW is, how it works, when to use it, what assumptions it makes, and what trade-offs or failure modes it has.
